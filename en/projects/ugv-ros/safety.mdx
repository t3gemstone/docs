---
title: 'Battery and Operating Safety'
description: 'Rules for Li-Po battery use, charging, transport and safe operation of the robot'
---

The robot runs on a 3S Li-Po battery (11.1 V nominal, 12.6 V fully charged, 4200 mAh, 40C). Li-Po batteries are light
and powerful, but when mishandled they can swell, catch fire and are hard to extinguish. The rules on this page apply
to everyone who works with the robot.

## 1. Battery: Mandatory Rules

<Warning>
The robot's software does not measure the battery voltage. The only protection against over-discharging the battery is
the low-voltage alarm.
</Warning>

- **Attach a low-voltage alarm (low battery alarm / Li-Po checker) to every battery.** The alarm plugs into the
  battery's balance connector, monitors each cell separately and beeps below the configured threshold. Set the
  threshold to **3.5 V/cell** (about 10.5 V for 3S). When the alarm beeps, stop the robot immediately and remove the
  battery. A Li-Po cell is permanently damaged below 3.0 V and may swell on the next charge.
- **Keep the Li-Po battery in a Li-Po safety bag (safe bag) while transporting and charging it.** The bag does not stop
  a fire, but it contains the flames and slows their spread. Put only one battery in the bag and close the zipper.
- **Charge the battery according to the documentation of its manufacturer (label, datasheet, user manual).** Charge
  current, charge voltage, temperature range and storage conditions vary between batteries; the manufacturer's values
  take precedence over the general values on this page. Also follow the instructions in the charger's manual.
- **Charge only with a balancing Li-Po charger** (balance connector attached, "LiPo Balance" mode, cell count 3S). The
  charge current must be at most 1C, i.e. **4.2 A** for a 4200 mAh battery; a lower current (e.g. 2 A) is better for
  battery life. The **40C** rating on the battery is the discharge rating (up to about 168 A can be drawn), not the
  charge current.
- **Never leave charging unattended** and never leave a battery charging overnight. Charge on a non-flammable surface
  (ceramic, concrete, metal tray) and away from flammable materials.
- **Do not use or charge a battery that is swollen, crushed, punctured, has damaged wires or is getting hot.** Put it
  in a safety bag, leave it outdoors in a non-flammable place and take it to a battery recycling point.

## 2. Battery: Daily Use

| State | Per cell | 3S total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fully charged | 4.20 V | 12.6 V |
| Storage | 3.80–3.85 V | 11.4–11.6 V |
| Alarm threshold (recommended) | 3.50 V | 10.5 V |
| Never go below | 3.00 V | 9.0 V |

- If you will not use the battery for more than a week, bring it to storage voltage with the charger's **Storage**
  mode. Storing a battery fully charged or empty shortens its life.
- The battery is warm right after driving; do not charge it before it cools down.
- If there is more than 0.1 V difference between cells, equalize them with a balance charge. If the difference keeps
  growing, the battery is aging.
- Make field adjustments (`min_duty`, `max_wheel_speed`) with a **fully charged battery**; with a weak battery the motors
  turn slower at the same duty value. [Field Test](/en/projects/ugv-ros/field-test#5-speed-range)
- In the cold (below 0 °C) battery capacity drops and the alarm goes off early. Do not charge a cold battery.

## 3. Transport

- Transport the battery **removed** from the robot and inside a safety bag. A battery carried inside the robot may
  short-circuit at its connector.
- Do not leave the connector (XT60, etc.) exposed; fit an end cap or tape it. Do not put the battery in the same pocket
  as keys, coins or tools.
- **On airplanes**, Li-Po batteries may only be carried in cabin baggage, not in checked baggage. Batteries below 100 Wh
  usually do not require approval (this battery: 11.1 V × 4.2 Ah ≈ 47 Wh). Each battery must be in a separate safety
  bag with its terminals insulated; check the airline's rules.
- Do not leave the battery in a vehicle parked in the sun.

## 4. In Case of Fire

- If you can, move the battery and the robot away from flammable materials. Do not inhale the smoke; Li-Po smoke is
  toxic.
- For small Li-Po fires, the most effective method is **cooling with plenty of water** or smothering with sand; a
  dry-powder (ABC) fire extinguisher can also be used. The battery may reignite after the fire is out; leave it outdoors
  in a bucket for several hours.

## 5. While Operating the Robot

- **Before connecting the battery**, check the wiring: polarity (red +, black −), loose or exposed ends, stray wire
  strands at the L298N screw terminals. A battery connected in reverse will burn the L298N and T3 Gemstone O1.
- **Remove the battery when changing wires.** Changing pins while the battery is connected can damage the GPIOs.
- A **main switch and fuse** (e.g. a 10 A blade fuse) on the battery line is recommended. A 40C battery can deliver more
  than 150 A in a short circuit; the fuse blows before the wiring and the L298N burn.
- **Supply T3 Gemstone O1's DC power input within the 5–12 V range.** A fully charged 3S battery reaches 12.6 V. To power
  T3 Gemstone O1 from this battery, feed the power input through a suitable DC regulator that can supply the board's current requirement.
  [T3 Gemstone O1 specifications](/en/boards/o1/introduction)
- **Never connect the battery to the Type-C input.** The Type-C input is for 5–9 V; the eFuse on this line cuts off at
  about 10.2 V.
- The Type-C input can be connected while the battery is attached. The board is designed as a priority power selector
  with two eFuses; if valid power is present on Type-C, it disables the battery path by itself. In this case the board
  is powered from USB and the motors from the battery.
- The battery input's undervoltage cutoff turns on at about 4.0 V and off at 3.6 V; in other words, the board **does not
  protect the battery from over-discharge**. Keeping the 3S battery above 10.5 V is the job of the low-voltage alarm.
- Run **separate wires** from the battery to T3 Gemstone O1 and to the L298N (star connection); do not bridge from one
  terminal to the other. Add a 470–1000 µF electrolytic capacitor at the L298N supply input; it suppresses the voltage
  fed back into the supply line when the motors stop suddenly. The battery, L298N, T3 Gemstone O1 and encoder **GNDs are
  common**.
- Run the first tests with the **wheels in the air** (by placing the robot on a box). This way, even if a software
  setting is wrong, the robot will not fall off the table.
- Keep your hands away from the wheels while the robot is moving, and know where the **EMERGENCY STOP**
  button is in the web interface. If the interface connection drops, the robot stops by itself due to the `/cmd_vel`
  timeout; however, do not rely on this to drive the robot near the edge of a table.
- The lidar brake (`safety.enabled`) only detects obstacles at the height the lidar sees; it does not see low objects,
  stairwells or table edges.
- The L298N heats up under load; do not touch its heat sink or block its ventilation.
- The lidar's laser is Class 1 (considered eye-safe), but still do not stare at the spinning head from close up for a
  long time.
- Do not drive the robot into water, onto wet floors, or over dusty surfaces or surfaces with metal shavings.

## 6. Shutting Down

Pulling the battery or the USB-C cable directly can corrupt the file system. Shut down the operating system first.

```bash
ssh -t gemstone@<robot-ip> sudo poweroff
```

Remove the battery after the board's lights go off (about 15 seconds). Since the board is powered from the battery, it
keeps drawing current from the battery even after `poweroff`. Do not leave the battery connected to the robot; within a
few hours it can drop below 3.0 V/cell and be permanently damaged.

## 7. Checklist

Before each session:

- Battery is not swollen, its wires are intact and its voltage is above 11.4 V
- Low-voltage alarm is attached to the balance connector and its threshold is 3.5 V
- Wiring and polarity checked, battery connected last
- Emergency stop button is open on the screen
- First movement tested with the wheels in the air

After the session:

- `sudo poweroff` executed, battery removed after the lights went off
- After cooling down, battery charged in a safety bag according to the manufacturer's instructions, or brought to
  storage voltage
